How it's made

Pipeline notes

The idea

Real-time renderers cannot afford to trace bounced light. So the slow, beautiful part is computed once in Blender and stored in textures; the browser replays it at 60fps and adds only what has to be live — sunlight, reflections, glow and the fire.

Geometry

Every object is generated procedurally by a Python script against Blender's bmesh API — no modelling by hand. Walls, joinery, the sofa's cushions, the globe's meridian ring and the plant's leaves are all code, so the room can be re-proportioned by changing a number. Vessels — the bowl, the mug, the planter — are lathed from 2-D profiles, so they are hollow and have a real lip rather than a capped cylinder's flat disc.

Materials

Textures are written pixel by pixel with NumPy: oak grain stretched along each board, plain-weave linen built from interleaved thread profiles, travertine with vermiculation and sedimentary banding. Normal maps come from the height fields through a five-tap filter, so relief reads without speckle.

The globe is real: Natural Earth coastlines and borders drawn to an equirectangular parchment map, with Lithuania picked out in gold leaf.

Light, baked and live

Eight channels were path-traced separately into one shared UV atlas — sky, three sun positions, lamp, moonlight, fire, and ambient occlusion gathered at 42 cm so a sealed room doesn't bake solid black.

Every frame, those channels are composited on the GPU into a single lightmap with live weights. Dragging the time of day re-mixes the bake while a real shadow-mapped sun swings overhead, so window bars throw crisp shadows the atlas could never hold. Closing the curtains attenuates the daylight channels directly.

Values exceed 1.0, so the atlas ships RGBM-encoded and is decoded during the composite. The multiplier rides in the alpha channel, which is why the lightmaps are lossless WebP while the material textures are lossy — a quantised multiplier would band the radiance badly.

Live on top

  • God rays: a thresholded radial blur masked to the window
  • Dust that only lights inside the shaft, and parts around the cursor
  • A pull cord simulated as a verlet chain — grab it, it stretches and snaps back
  • Sparks with buoyancy and drag when the fire is poked
  • An environment probe captured from inside the room, refreshed when the light changes
  • Room sound synthesised in WebAudio — filtered noise and scheduled crackles, no audio files
  • A wall clock on the hour you are actually reading this: the dial and case are baked, only the hands are live
  • Steam off the mug that thins out as the tea goes cold

Two ways to look

An orbit camera turns around a fixed pivot, which frames a room well and explores it badly — you are held at one point and spun. Walk puts you inside at eye height: drag to look, WASD or the thumbstick to move. Collision was almost free, because the physics layer already knows where the floor, the walls and the furniture are; a capsule is resolved against the same boxes the props fall onto.

The visitor book

A portfolio room is read-only by nature: people look, then leave, and you never know they came. The book on the shelf runs the other way. It is open to anyone — no sign-in, no email — because a book you have to register for is a book nobody signs.

Entries live in Firestore, reached over its REST API rather than the Firebase SDK: no hundred-kilobyte dependency and no build step. Anonymous auth gives each visitor an id without asking them for anything, and every constraint that matters — one row per person, create and edit your own only, never delete, field lengths pinned — is enforced in the security rules, where a client cannot argue with it.

Things that fall over

The loose objects are real rigid bodies. Tap one and it takes the impulse where you touched it, so it topples rather than wobbles; drag one and it follows your pointer on a constraint, keeping the momentum when you let go. Books can be pulled straight off the shelf.

A baked lightmap assumes nothing moves, so the solver is lazy: a body exists only while its object is away from home, the world steps only while something is awake, and an object that settles goes back to being ordinary static geometry. As a thing leaves the spot it was baked in, its share of the bake fades out and the environment probe takes over. Tidy up puts everything back.

Soft furnishings

Cushions are not rounded boxes. Each one is blended toward its circumscribed ellipsoid so every panel bulges, then the eight sewn corners are drawn back in to give the little ears a stuffed cushion has, and the seat cushions carry a dip from being sat on. Welt cord is swept along the arm seams.

The throws are not modelled at all — they are dropped onto the real sofa geometry and simulated with Blender's cloth solver, then frozen where they landed, so the folds are the ones the fabric actually fell into.

Controls

  • Drag to orbit; scroll or pinch to pull back — past the walls the lens widens instead
  • Tap the markers, or the hearth, the cord, the globe and the books directly
  • Tap a loose object to knock it over, or drag it anywhere; pull books off the shelf
  • Walk to step inside; drag to look, W A S D to move
  • Tidy up returns everything to where it started
  • 1–5 views, F fire, L lamp, C curtains, G globe, H hide the controls, Esc back
  • Append ?time=0.9&view=fire&weather=rain to share a state
